    About us

    Many people stand behind the Curriculum Ontology (Lehrplan-Ontologie): curriculum experts from the federal states, a project team at the FWU, experts in ontologies and knowledge graphs, and an international community whose open standards and tools make this work possible in the first place.

    Together we pursue a goal that becomes very concrete in everyday school life: teachers should be able to quickly find suitable educational media for the competences and contents of their curriculum, and the curricula of sixteen federal states should be understandable and comparable in such a way that their diversity remains visible while their commonalities still become apparent.

    The FWU – the media institute of the federal states

    The FWU Institut für Film und Bild in Wissenschaft und Unterricht (Institute for Film and Image in Science and Education) was founded as a joint institution of the federal states and has supported teaching at schools throughout Germany for more than 75 years, from the film reel to the digital learning platform. Today it pursues two goals: to develop high-quality educational media for direct use in the classroom and, together with the federal states, to build a future-proof educational media infrastructure that makes learning content permanently accessible. The Curriculum Ontology is one building block of this infrastructure.

    The MEM project

    The Curriculum Ontology is being developed in the project MEM (Metadata for Educational Media), which the sixteen federal states initiated as part of the DigitalPakt Schule (Digital Pact for Schools). MEM extends SODIX, the educational media infrastructure shared by the federal states, through which teaching materials are held centrally and provided to the media libraries of the federal states. There, the materials are described with extensive metadata; MEM adds references to the competences and contents of the curricula. In the long term, this is intended to give teachers a search that is oriented toward the curricula of their federal state instead of leaving them alone with long lists of results.

    The kick-off was a workshop at the Didacta education fair in Cologne in February 2024. Since then, the project has been in dialogue with the federal states, with sister projects of the FWU and with the professional community, for example in workshops on the topic "Maschinenlesbarkeit und Lehrpläne" (machine readability and curricula).

    The beginnings in the IT’s JOINTLY project

    The first, prototypical idea for the Curriculum Ontology emerged in the project IT’s JOINTLY, which was funded by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research and the European Union. The project is developing a solution based on artificial intelligence (AI) with which educational content can be described with metadata, curated and made findable. As one of its results, yovisto GmbH developed a first Curriculum Ontology that relates school subjects, competences and learning contents to one another on the basis of the framework curricula (Rahmenlehrpläne) of Berlin and Brandenburg. The Lehrplan-Ontologie builds on this basic idea and extends it to the curricula of all federal states.

    The working group of the federal states

    An ontology for curricula can only succeed if it is shaped by those who know curricula and are responsible for them. The working group of curriculum experts from the federal states (Fachgruppe der Länder) has accompanied the project from the very beginning. Its members contribute the perspectives of their federal states, clarify content-related questions, classify curricular structures and check whether the modeling does justice to the curricula. Many decisions in this ontology, especially where the federal states take different paths, emerged from this exchange.

    yovisto

    The ontological modeling is carried out in close collaboration with ontology experts from yovisto GmbH. yovisto designs, develops and operates solutions for extracting knowledge from media content and making it accessible. In regular joint working sessions with the MEM team, the Curriculum Ontology was designed, tested and refined step by step.

    Acknowledgments

    We would like to thank everyone who has contributed to this work:

    • the members of the working group of the federal states for their patience, their knowledge and their willingness to look at their own curricula again and again from a new perspective,
    • the federal states, whose curricula and curriculum data form the basis of this work,
    • the Standing Conference of the Ministers of Education and Cultural Affairs (Kultusministerkonferenz, KMK) and the Institut zur Qualitätsentwicklung im Bildungswesen (IQB, Institute for Educational Quality Improvement) for the educational standards and for making them available in machine-readable form,
    • the working group Kompetenzzentrum Interoperable Metadaten (KIM, Competence Center for Interoperable Metadata) of the Deutsche Initiative für Netzwerkinformation (DINI, German Initiative for Network Information) for the shared vocabularies for school subjects and school types,
    • the communities behind the Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), the OBO Foundry (OBO: Open Biological and Biomedical Ontologies), the Ontology Development Kit (ODK) and Protégé, whose open work we are able to reuse,
    • and everyone who uses the ontology, reports errors and suggests improvements.

    Contact

    We welcome questions, feedback and ideas, whether about individual terms, the curriculum data of a federal state or new applications.

    • Questions about the project and the curriculum data: MEM editorial team (Redaktion MEM), redaktion@mem.schule
    • Errors and suggestions concerning the ontology: as an issue in the GitHub repository (see also Contributing)
    • News from the project: on the MEM website, where you can also subscribe to a newsletter

    We hope that the Curriculum Ontology makes working with curricula a little easier for many people – and that in the end it arrives where it belongs: in the classroom.
